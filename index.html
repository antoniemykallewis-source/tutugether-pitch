What we found / 01

Book Now startsin San Jose.

The main homepage Book Now button opens the Valley Fair / Santa Clara page. Its form starts with “Begin Valley Fair - San Jose Reservation.” Roseville is a separate link.

Why it matters. A Roseville visitor has to spot the location switch before choosing a craft. That adds a step; we have not measured any loss of bookings.

Our fix. Use one neutral studio chooser with clear city labels. Send Roseville ads and creator links straight to the Roseville booking page.

Observed homepage button and destination. The booking form did load after scrolling. We did not place a reservation.

What we found / 02

The TikTok linkhits a dead end.

The website links to @tutugether.ca. TikTok returned “Couldn’t find this account” for that exact address in this browser.

Why it matters. A customer following the site’s TikTok link cannot see the videos through that route. This does not prove the business has no TikTok account.

Our fix. Confirm the right handle, then replace that link in the footer and contact page. Test it while signed out.

Observed in a signed-out browser on November 6. Account availability can change.

What we found / 03

The search mappoints to a 404.

The robots.txt file tells search engines to use sitemap_indexs.xml. That exact address returns Page not found.

Why it matters. A sitemap is a list of pages for search engines. This broken route makes that list harder to find. We did not measure any effect on ranking.

Our fix. Point robots.txt to the working WordPress sitemap. Remove test and payment-result pages from the list, then check it in Search Console.

The working wp-sitemap.xml exists. This is not evidence that Google cannot index the site.

What we found / 04

About us opensa missing page.

The homepage and footer send About us visitors to /about/. That address returns a real 404 page.

Why it matters. Someone looking for the people behind the studio loses that path to trust. We cannot tell how often customers click it.

Our fix. Restore the page or update every About us link to the right page. Use real staff, studio and first-visit photos.

HTTP 404 and visible error page both checked. Other tested booking and contact pages returned 200.

What we found / 05

The SF pagestell two stories.

The San Francisco project page says “Coming soon.” The same site still lists an SF address, hours and booking link.

Why it matters. A visitor cannot be sure whether that studio is open or what they can make there. The actual operating status needs the owner’s confirmation.

Our fix. Confirm SF’s current status. Then give its project, contact and booking pages the same clear message.

This is a website content mismatch, not a claim that the SF studio is closed.

What we found / 06

Mixed craft groupsbook separately.

The Roseville booking page tells groups to submit separate reservations for tufting and decoden/pouring. Groups that exceed the available online openings are asked to email.

Why it matters. Party planners must coordinate more than one request. Some may need help keeping the whole group together. We have not measured the effect on bookings.

Our fix. Add one group inquiry path with location, date, headcount and craft choices. State when the team will reply. Keep the current policy clear until the flow changes.

This is a published booking policy, not a broken form. No form was submitted.
